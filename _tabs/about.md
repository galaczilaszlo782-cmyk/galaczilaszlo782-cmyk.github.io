Galaczi László-Alpár. Based in Munich.

## What this blog is

A working notebook. I publish technical writeups from labs, CTFs, and the topics I'm digging into outside of work — primarily HackTheBox Sherlocks (forensics and incident reconstruction), with longer pieces when a topic warrants it.

The angle is purple team. I'm interested in attack techniques and detection in equal measure. Posts often pair the offensive side — what the technique does, what it looks like on the wire or on disk — with the defensive view — what ends up in logs, what telemetry would catch it, what won't.

## Focus areas

**Cloud security.** Active learning track. Working through AWS and Azure security fundamentals, IAM and identity attack paths, and detection in cloud-native logging — CloudTrail, GuardDuty, Azure Activity logs, Defender for Cloud. Writeups will follow as I work through certifications and hands-on labs.

**AI security.** LLM application attack surface: prompt injection, indirect injection through tool and RAG inputs, data exfiltration paths in agentic systems, and the gap between model-level safety controls and application-level controls. Less mature than the other two tracks here — expect exploratory posts.

**Purple team.** Detection engineering and log analysis. Auth log forensics, brute force pattern analysis, lateral movement signatures, and the recurring question: what does this technique actually leave behind? The HackTheBox Sherlocks writeups live in this category.

## Background

MSc in Intelligent Systems. Several years working in network and infrastructure security in a financial services environment. Active on HackTheBox.

## On the writing

Posts are written for practitioners — security engineers, red and blue team members, and readers with hands-on experience. The content assumes working familiarity with operating systems, networking, and common security tooling, and prioritizes technical depth over introductory background. Commands, configurations, and code are included where they support the analysis. Attacker infrastructure is defanged where relevant.

Most posts are published in English. Selected pieces are also available in German.
